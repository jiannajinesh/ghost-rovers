The challenge

NASA has left rovers, landers and probes all over the solar system since the 1960s. Kids often hear only about today's missions. The challenge asks for a story-driven tool that introduces school-age space fans to this hardware and the science it made possible.

Our idea

Every machine is a character that tells its own story, in its own voice. Kids spin a 3D Moon and Mars, find the "ghosts" at their real landing spots, read and hear their diaries, take a quiz, and live Spirit's real adventure in a game.

How Ghost Rovers meets the challenge

Story-drivenEach machine speaks in first person: "Dear Diary, I landed in 1967…". Hosts Grandma Moon, Uncle Mars and Captain Comet guide the way. Real hardware, real places20 machines from the challenge resource list, pinned at their real landing coordinates on 3D globes. Engineering to scienceEvery diary card links "Why I was sent" to "What I found", like Spirit's broken wheel uncovering signs of ancient hot springs. Show, don't listIn the game, kids drive Spirit, fight dust, lose a wheel and get stuck at Troy, just as it really happened. Made for ages 10–14Simple words, funny characters, quick quizzes, a timeline and a certificate for meeting all 20. AccessibleEvery diary is read aloud. Machines can be opened from the lists without using the 3D view. Works with keyboard, mouse and touch. Voices that ageVoices change with age: 1960s–70s machines sound like old radio broadcasts, newer ones sound clear and fresh.

Data sources (NASA)

    Pictures

    • Real pictures: mission photos and artwork collected by our team from the web.
    • 3D model images: made by our team with Meshy AI, using real hardware as reference.
    • Moon and Mars globes: drawn by code at real map positions (maria, volcanoes, craters, ice caps).

    Built with, and use of AI

    • HTML, CSS, JavaScript, three.js (3D) and the Web Audio API (sound effects).
    • Voices: Kokoro, an open-source text-to-speech model, with age effects added by our team.
    • AI help: Claude (Anthropic) helped write code and drafts; Meshy AI made the 3D model images.
    • Facts are based on the NASA and NSSDCA pages listed above.

    Team

    A team of young space explorers, aged 10 to 14, from Kerala, India.
